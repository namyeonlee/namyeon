Teaching Philosophy

“You teach me, I forget. You show me, I remember. You involve me, I understand.” — E.O. Wilson

This quote resonates with how I approach teaching: students learn best when they are actively involved. My classes are designed to move beyond lectures, bringing concepts to life through real-world projects, client collaborations, and professional simulations. Whether students are role-playing a crisis scenario, developing a public relations campaign for a local nonprofit, or experimenting with AI tools in research, they learn by doing—building not only knowledge but also adaptability, creativity, and confidence. I continually update my courses with current industry practices and emerging technologies, ensuring students graduate prepared to meet the demands of today’s fast-changing communication and media landscape.

Courses I Teach

Introduction to Public Relations (PRE 2200)
Overview of PR history, principles, ethics, and trends
  • Activities: news briefings, crisis simulations, career workshops
Social Media for Public Relations (PRE 3600)
Creating, managing, and evaluating social media campaigns
  • Trend analysis and semester-long brand projects
Public Relations Case Studies (PRE 4090)
Online course exploring real PR cases
  • Analyze situations, evaluate strategies, and propose solutions through interactive discussion
Public Relations Campaigns (PRE 4600 / PRE 4900)
Capstone: client-based professional PR campaigns
  • Students work with real clients to research, design, and present campaigns
  • Recent project: partnered with Hope Alive, a local nonprofit addressing substance use, to create a health communication campaign
  • Students conducted primary/secondary research, developed targeted strategies to improve awareness and outreach, and presented a professional plan book—delivering community impact and portfolio-ready work
Mass Communication Theory and Research (MCM 4360)
Key theories and research methods in mass communication
  • Hands-on projects and AI-based simulations
